It has become common in professional, academic and personal settings to generate summaries of meetings from that meeting’s audio. In this project we will be looking into how using a participant’s meeting notes can improve the quality of the summary, and if it can compensate for errors caused by an incorrect automatic transcription of the meeting.
Meeting summarization has been extensively studied, but this particular project improves on a known research gap because it uses multiple sources to generate a summary: the meeting transcription and a participant’s notes. Analysing the quality of a summary is a difficult task. There are many metrics and methods for evaluation, as well as many ways in which an error in the source, here the transcription, can influence the quality of the summary.
For this project we use the AMI corpus, a well-studied dataset containing audio files, transcriptions, summaries and participant notes, and a light version of OpenAI’s “Whisper”, a tool that generates transcriptions from audio files. We will use an AI model to generate summaries. We will analyse how good a generated summary is by comparing it with the corresponding summary provided in the corpus. To do so we will calculate scores like ROUGE, BART or BERT.
We will start by generating transcriptions, using Whisper on audio files that contain a lot of background noise and some unintelligible voices. The resulting text will be “noisy”, it will contain many errors, and we will calculate how many incorrect or missing words there are in the generated transcriptions, compared to the corresponding correct transcription in the corpus.
Then, we will use our chosen AI model to generate summaries on the corpus’ transcriptions as well as those the “noisy” ones we have generated. Finally, we will generate summaries by giving both a transcription and the participant notes to our AI model, for both types of transcriptions. This will show us the impact of automatic transcription errors and of the notes, respectively, on the quality of the summary.
